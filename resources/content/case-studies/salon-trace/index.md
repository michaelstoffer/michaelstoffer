## Overview

Salon Trace is a salon in Delray Beach, Florida, with a service offering that spans color, styling, extensions, and Head Spa experiences. The website needed to make that range approachable while presenting a consistent, considered brand.

I designed and developed the website with a practical goal: help someone understand what the salon offers, explore the services that interest them, and know how to take the next step. The work included the public experience, service content, and the hosting and infrastructure needed to support the site.

## Designing Around Service Discovery

A service website has to answer useful questions before it asks someone to book. What is available? How do the options differ? Where can a new client ask for guidance?

I organized the experience around those decisions:

- A homepage that introduces the salon and leads into its services.
- Dedicated service pages for visitors who want more detail.
- A prominent Head Spa section with individual treatment options.
- Gallery and team pages that let visitors explore the salon's work and people.
- Contact and appointment inquiry paths accessible throughout the site.

The design connects imagery, typography, and clear navigation across those pages. On smaller screens, the same priorities remain: readable content, understandable choices, and an accessible next step.

## Connecting Interest to an Inquiry

The site supports different entry points rather than assuming everyone arrives ready to choose an appointment. A visitor can begin with a service, browse the gallery, read about the team, or go directly to contact.

Service-specific links carry context into the contact page. For example, the Head Spa inquiry path identifies the visitor's area of interest instead of sending every visitor through an identical starting point.

These are inquiry paths: the salon still handles availability and appointment confirmation. The interface should help a client start a useful conversation without promising a confirmed appointment it cannot guarantee.

## Consultation Tools with a Clear Role

The public site also presents a hairstyle preview and a booking assistant. These tools support exploration: visitors can consider a look or seek guidance about the available services before speaking with the salon.

They complement the consultation process. A generated preview is a starting point for discussion, and service guidance does not replace a stylist's judgment or confirm availability.

## Delivery Beyond the Page

The project also involved hosting and launch infrastructure. A finished interface needs a dependable path to production and a foundation that can support subsequent content and service changes.

The application uses Laravel with Vue, Inertia, and Tailwind CSS. That keeps the public experience within a cohesive application rather than splitting routine content, navigation, and inquiry flows across disconnected implementations.

## The Delivered Result

The live site brings the salon's services, Head Spa offering, visual work, team, and contact paths into one experience. Visitors can explore the offering and move into an inquiry from the pages relevant to them.

## What This Demonstrates

- Translating a service business into a clear website structure.
- Connecting visual design with practical navigation and inquiry flows.
- Delivering a custom Laravel and Vue application from design through launch.
- Presenting consultation tools with appropriate expectations.
- Treating hosting and ongoing ownership as part of delivery.
